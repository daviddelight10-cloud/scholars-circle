# The Science of Spaced Repetition: Why Cramming Doesn't Work

You've been there. Exam is tomorrow. You read the entire textbook in one sitting. You feel prepared. Then you open the question paper and... nothing.

Here's why that happens — and what to do instead.

## Why Cramming Fails

Cramming feels productive because of **fluency illusion**. When you read something repeatedly in a short time, it feels familiar. But familiarity is not the same as understanding.

### The Problem with Massed Practice

When you cram, you're doing what psychologists call **massed practice** — studying one topic intensely in a single session. Research consistently shows:

- Massed practice produces **short-term fluency** but poor long-term retention
- Students who cram score well on immediate tests but fail delayed tests
- The information doesn't transfer to exam day because it was never consolidated

## How Memory Actually Works

### Three Stages of Memory

1. **Encoding** — Your brain takes in information from your senses
2. **Consolidation** — The information is stabilized and stored
3. **Retrieval** — You access the stored information later

Consolidation happens **between study sessions**, not during them. This is why spacing matters — you need gaps for your brain to consolidate.

### The Spacing Effect

Hermann Ebbinghaus discovered the spacing effect in 1885. He found that:

- **Spaced learning** (reviewing over multiple sessions) produces **200–400% better retention** than massed learning
- Each review strengthens the memory trace
- The optimal interval increases as the memory gets stronger

## The Evidence

### Studies on Spaced Repetition

- **Cepeda et al. (2006)** — Meta-analysis of 254 studies. Spacing effect was found in **every single one**.
- **Rohrer & Taylor (2007)** — Students who spaced their math practice scored **62% higher** on a delayed test than those who massed it.
- **Sobel et al. (2011)** — Even a single spaced review dramatically improved retention over cramming.

### Why It Works

1. **Retrieval practice** — Each review forces your brain to retrieve the information, strengthening the memory
2. **Consolidation time** — Gaps between sessions allow your brain to consolidate memories during sleep
3. **Desirable difficulty** — Forgetting slightly and then recalling makes the memory stronger

## How to Space Your Reviews

### The Ideal Schedule

| Review | When |
|--------|------|
| 1st | Immediately after learning |
| 2nd | 1 day later |
| 3rd | 3 days later |
| 4th | 1 week later |
| 5th | 2 weeks later |
| 6th | 1 month later |

This is roughly what the SM-2 algorithm does — but it adjusts based on your performance.
